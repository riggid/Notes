> [!question] Explain the Kelvin-Planck and Clausius statements of the Second Law of Thermodynamics and provide a real-world example of each.
>> [!success]- Answer
>> The Kelvin-Planck statement says it's impossible for a heat engine operating in a cycle to receive heat from a single reservoir and produce a net amount of work. An example is the impossibility of creating a perfect heat engine that converts all heat into work. The Clausius statement says it's impossible to construct a device that produces no effect other than the transfer of heat from a cooler body to a hotter body. A real-world example is that a refrigerator requires work input to transfer heat from the cold interior to the warmer surroundings.

> [!question] What is the difference between heat and work in the context of thermodynamics?
>> [!success]- Answer
>> Heat is energy transferred due to a temperature difference, while work is energy transferred that is not heat.

> [!question] A closed system undergoes a cycle consisting of four processes. The total heat transfer for the cycle is -170 kJ. The system completes 100 cycles per minute.  Calculate the net rate of work output in kW.  Show your working.
>> [!success]- Answer
>> The net heat transfer rate is -170 kJ/cycle * 100 cycles/min = -17000 kJ/min.  For a thermodynamic cycle, the net heat transfer equals the net work. Therefore, the net work output rate is -17000 kJ/min. Converting to kW: (-17000 kJ/min)*(1 min/60 s) = -283.3 kW. The negative sign indicates that work is done by the system.

> [!question] A 1.5 kg mass of air is compressed isothermally from 0.1 MPa to 0.7 MPa, following $PV = Constant$. The initial density is $1.16 kg/m^3$. Find the work done by the system.  Show your working.
>> [!success]- Answer
>> The initial volume ($V_1$) is the mass divided by density:  $V_1 = \u2248 1.293 m^3$. The isothermal work is given by $W = p_1 V_1 \ln(p_1 / p_2)$.  Substituting, we get $W \approx -251.6 kJ$. The negative sign indicates work done on the system.

>> [!success]- Answer
>> pressure, temperature, volume

> [!question] Describe the key differences between the three main types of hybrid electric vehicles (HEVs): series, parallel, and series-parallel.  Explain the advantages and disadvantages of each type and provide a suitable real-world example of a vehicle that could represent each type.
>> [!success]- Answer
>> Series HEVs use the internal combustion engine (ICE) solely to power a generator that in turn powers an electric motor. Advantages are simple design and good efficiency at low speeds. Disadvantages are lower overall efficiency at higher speeds. A real-world example would be an older generation hybrid. Parallel HEVs have both the ICE and electric motor connected to the wheels, allowing either or both to power the vehicle. Advantages are good efficiency at high and low speeds. Disadvantages are more complex design.  A common example is the Toyota Prius. Series-parallel HEVs combine elements of both series and parallel systems. Advantages are flexibility, potentially higher efficiency. Disadvantages are the most complex design.  Many modern hybrids employ series-parallel systems.
